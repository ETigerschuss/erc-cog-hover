## Research independence and the capacity to deliver HOVER

My work spans the levels HOVER connects: cellular physiology of fly motion vision (first-author
*Neuron* 2016; *Nature Neuroscience* 2016, 2023), connectomics and research software (MESH, the
electrical gaze pathway), evolution (the origin of insect eye movements and the male-only third
retinal muscle), differentiable modelling and machine learning (stimulus optimisation, segmentation
of terabyte EM volumes, TinyML on robots), human gaze (first-author eye-tracking work at LMU) and
participatory science (first and senior-author publications, a national jury, international invited
talks). The independent lines are visible: MESH is my own tool, the citizen-science programme is my
own, the eye-tracking study is my own, and the gap-junction search strategy that made the electrical
gaze pathway findable was my design. HOVER is a new programme defined and led from my group at IU. It
moves from describing circuits to asking *why* circuits differ between the sexes, in a new species,
with connectome-constrained spiking models, neuromorphic machines and public experiments as the
instruments. It is complementary to, and agreed with, the Fenk laboratory's *Drosophila* programme on
eye-movement mechanisms. The partner groups contribute set-ups and expertise; the team members at the
partner sites are selected, funded and directed by me.
